1. The big idea — how the whole thing works

Before any button makes sense, you need the shape of the system. It is simpler than it looks.

In one paragraph: The platform watches the option market every minute. Three "filter" engines each look at the data and say CALL, PUT, or nothing. If they agree (you choose what "agree" means), the system starts hunting. A fourth engine — Ultra Master Pro — then decides the exact moment to buy, and later the exact moment to sell. Everything else on these screens is you telling those four engines how to behave.

The four engines, and the one rule that matters

EngineTab nameIts one job
OI StructureOI ChangeReads the shape of open-interest movement. Votes CALL / PUT / nothing.
MTF RatioMulti-TFCompares call vs put OI across ten timeframes. Votes CALL / PUT / nothing.
MQAERatioScores five models on the PCR and ratio lines. Votes CALL / PUT / nothing.
Ultra Master ProUltra Master ProDoes not vote. It executes — picks the entry moment, and owns every exit.
The rule to remember: the first three decide whether to trade and in which direction. Ultra Master Pro decides when to get in and when to get out. Once a trade is open, the first three stop mattering entirely — if they all flip to the opposite direction mid-trade, nothing happens. Only Ultra Master Pro's exit ladder (or the day's End-Exit) closes a position.

Day → Zone → Engine

Settings are organised in three nested levels. Almost every screen is somewhere in this tree:

WeekdayMonday to Friday. Each weekday has its own index (NIFTY, SENSEX…), its own money limits, and its own on/off switch. They are completely independent — Monday's settings have nothing to do with Tuesday's.
ZoneEach weekday has three time windows: Z1, Z2, Z3. A zone is a slice of the trading day (e.g. 09:20–10:30) with its own price band, its own trade limit, and its own engine settings.
Engine settingsInside each zone, all four engines have their own full parameter set. So the same engine can behave differently in Z1 and Z3 on the same day.

That means there are 5 weekdays × 3 zones = 15 independent zone configurations, each holding four complete engine setups. This is why the Copy tools (section 7) exist.

3. How saving works — read this before touching anything

Nothing you click is live until you press Save. Every change — including kill switches — goes into a local draft. The engine keeps running on the last saved version until you commit.
DraftYour unsaved edits. The top bar shows "3 unsaved changes" in amber.
DiscardThrows the draft away and returns to the saved version. No confirmation box — it is instant.
Save…Opens the confirmation dialog showing exactly which fields change, old value → new value.
VersionEvery save creates a numbered version (v41, v42…). Nothing is ever overwritten; you can restore any earlier one.

The confirmation dialog

Titled "Are you sure you want to submit these settings?", it shows "N field(s) will change. The save is atomic — confirmed changes are written as one new config version and audited field-by-field." and a three-column table: Setting · Old · New. Field names are written in plain form — days.monday.zones.Z1.premium_max appears as "Monday · Z1 · premium max". You can add an optional note for the history.

You can turn this dialog off in Security & Backups → Config Lock. The save that turns it off is itself still confirmed — deliberately.

If someone else saved while you were editing, your save is refused (error 409), the dialog closes, and the latest config is reloaded so you can re-apply your edits on top. Nothing is silently overwritten — ever.

6. Daily Trading Config

The main control room. Top to bottom:

6.1 Master Kill Switch

A red-bordered panel at the very top.

The rule for every kill switch — Master, Day and Zone all work the same way:
  1. Switch ON while a trade is running → that trade is exited at market the moment you save. If the broker rejects the sell, the engine keeps retrying every minute and sends a critical alert.
  2. For as long as it stays ON → no new trade is taken.
  3. Switch OFF → trading resumes from the next minute. No restart needed.

What each one covers: Master Kill — everything. Day kill — today's running trade (including one carried in from yesterday) and every zone today. Zone kill — the trade that zone opened, and new entries in that zone.

The trade's exit reason shows as Master Kill, Day Kill or Zone Kill in the trade list and the Telegram alert, and the save message tells you which trade was exited.

6.2 Overnight Carry

An amber panel. Two very different behaviours:

What happens
ON (default)Exactly like TradingView. Only Ultra Master Pro's exit ladder closes a position. It stays open overnight, End-Exit is ignored, and the only automatic close is the expiry-day 15:25 force-close.
OFFThe position is force-closed at the end of the day (End-Exit).
While this is ON, every zone's End-Exit switch is greyed out and labelled "End-Exit ignored". That is not a bug — carry overrides it.

6.3 Live Decision Snapshot

Refreshes every 5 seconds. Six tiles: Engine State, Active Zone, Direction, Trades Today, Day P&L, Position. Below them, one badge per engine showing its current vote. An amber line "⚠ …" lists anything currently blocking trading. If the engine has paused itself you get a red "⏸ reason" and a Resume engine button.

If the card is missing entirely, the engine loop is not running in this backend (replay mode, or not started).

6.4 Master Kill Grid

A 5×4 grid of coloured dots — the fastest way to see what is switched on. Rows are Monday–Friday; columns are Z1 · Z2 · Z3 · Day. Red = killed, green = live. Click any dot to toggle it. Hover to read "Monday Z1: live".

6.5 Account & Position Sizing

Demat Account Balance (₹)Default 30000. A placeholder until the broker supplies it live.
Paper Trading ModeSend orders to the simulator instead of the broker. Same switch as the Paper tab.
How position size is decided (verbatim): "No minimum capital. Each day picks All-In (100% of remaining balance) or an allocation %. Lots at entry = allocated capital ÷ (live premium × lot size) — never a fixed input. Max concurrent positions: 1 (fixed)."

So you never type a lot count anywhere. You set a budget, and the system works out the lots from the live price. And you can only ever hold one position at a time.

6.8 Indicator Settings

One block per zone. This is where you choose which engines vote and how their votes are counted.

ReevalEvery candle (re-decide each minute) or Once at start (decide at the zone open and stick with it).
HoldExperimental. Keeps a hunt alive this many minutes through brief "no direction" flickers. An opposite signal still cancels immediately. 0 = strict. Allowed 0–375.
Each engine rowA switch (vote / don't vote), a strikes dropdown (ATM ±5 / ±10 / ±15 / ±20 / All strikes) controlling how much of the chain that engine sums, a ↗ link to its dashboard, and a ⧉ button to copy its parameters elsewhere.

The Combine row — how votes become a decision

CombineUnanimous: every switched-on engine that has a direction must agree. Majority: the side with more votes wins; a tie is No Trade.
Neutral =Blocker: a switched-on engine with no direction stops the trade. Abstention: it is ignored and the others decide.
If you pick Majority with an even number of engines enabled, an amber note appears: "2 enabled — a split vote ties, and a tie is No Trade". Majority works best with an odd number.

Below the row, a sentence tells you the current rule in words — e.g. "Rule: 3 of 3 enabled — all must agree on CALL or PUT, otherwise No Trade. A switched-on indicator with no direction blocks the trade." Always read this line after changing anything here.

Strategy Active

On the Ultra Master Pro row. Turning it off puts the zone in observation mode: the filters still run and record their signals, but the execution engine is locked — no hunts, no entries.

7. The Copy Settings dialog

The safe way to copy, because it shows you exactly what will change first.

  1. Choose what to copy — whole day, one zone, or one indicator.
  2. Choose the source — which day, which zone, which engine.
  3. Choose targets — tick cells in a grid, or use the quick pills ("all days", "all 15", "same zone on all days"). The source cell is disabled so you cannot copy onto itself.
  4. Read the preview — one expandable row per target: "▸ Tuesday Z1 — 14 fields change" or "— already identical". Expand to see every field, old value in red, new in green.
  5. Copy into draft — still needs a Save.
Watch for the amber ⚠ lines. Two warnings can appear:
  • "Index changes: Tuesday (SENSEX) will switch to NIFTY — the whole day copies, including its index."
  • "Index mismatch: NIFTY parameters onto Tuesday (SENSEX) — strike windows and premium bands may not suit that index."
Both matter: NIFTY and SENSEX have different price levels, so a band that works for one may be empty for the other.

In whole-day scope there is a tick box "Keep each target day's own zone start/end times", off by default — meaning a day copy replaces the times too.

8. Engine — OI Change (OI Structure)

Full name on screen: "OI Structure Engine — the 'OI Change' entry filter".

What it does in one line: it treats open-interest movement like a price chart — looking for higher highs, lower lows and breakouts — scores what it finds, and votes CALL or PUT if the score clears a threshold.

8.2 Engine Master Switches

1-Minute Structure EngineOn: higher-high / lower-low patterns and range breakouts contribute. Off: 1-minute structure is excluded from scoring entirely.
5-Minute Red Candle RuleOn: the red-candle rule contributes. Off: red candles add nothing regardless of colour.

Turning both off means this engine can never produce a signal.

8.3 Global Aggregation Result (read-only)

A big CALL / PUT / NO_TRADE badge, the two point totals with a bar, and a plain-English verdict — either "Confidence gate: leading side (42 pts) is below the 55 threshold → NO_TRADE" or "Confidence gate: CALL leads with 70 pts, clearing 55".

8.7 Swing Scoring Lookback

One box: Lookback (bars, 0 = whole session), default 0, allowed 0–375.

0"Whole session. Every swing confirmed since the open keeps scoring, so the point totals climb all day and the 55 threshold is cleared easily by the afternoon. This is the original behaviour."
e.g. 30"Only swings confirmed in the last 30 bars score, which keeps the 55 threshold meaningful late in the session. The range zone and the breakout test still read the complete structure."
Why this exists. With 0, points only ever accumulate — by lunchtime the total is in the hundreds and the threshold stops filtering anything. A lookback makes the score reflect recent structure. Crucially it only filters scoring: the range zone and breakout test still see the whole day, so a short lookback never erases a level formed this morning.

8.8 Candle Rule

The panel spells out the active choice underneath, e.g. "Reading 5-bar candle, last closed."

9. Engine — Multi-TF (MTF Ratio)

On screen: "MTF Ratio — the 'Multi-TF' entry filter".

In one line: it measures how call and put open interest changed over ten different time windows, then runs your rules against those numbers. It is the only engine you program yourself.

9.1 The table

Columns: Timeframe · Call OI Δ · Put OI Δ · Call : Put · Lowest OI Side.

"Lowest OI Side" is the key column. It is the side with the lower signed change — and it is what the rules filter on. It is deliberately counter-intuitive: the side people are not piling into is often the direction that follows.

9.2 Timeframe rows computed

Ten pills: 1 Min · 3 Min · 5 Min · 10 Min · 15 Min · 30 Min · 1 Hr · 2 Hrs · 3 Hrs · Full Day. All on by default. Switching one off removes that row — and any rule that references it shows "(not computed)" beside the timeframe.

9.3 Writing rules

Each rule is: a name, an on/off switch, a THEN result (Call / Put / Neutral), and a list of conditions joined by AND. Rules run top to bottom; the first rule that fully matches wins and evaluation stops.

A condition reads left to right:

ColumnChoicesMeaning
Timeframeany of the tenWhich row to test.
Lowest-OI SideAny / Call / PutWhich side must be the lower one.
OperatorAny / Above / BelowHow to compare the ratio. "Above" catches capitulation (a wide imbalance); "Below" catches tight ranges; "Any" ignores size.
ThresholdAny, 1:1, 1:1.2, 1:1.5, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, or customThe ratio to compare against. Choose "custom… (type 1:N)" to type e.g. 1:2.5. The box border turns red until the format is valid; press Enter to commit. The ▾ button returns to the preset list.
Call Sign / Put SignAny / Positive (+) / Negative (−)Whether that side's change must be rising or falling.
A rule with no conditions shows "No conditions — this rule can never match". Delete removes a whole rule and ✕ removes one condition — neither asks for confirmation, but both are only draft changes until you Save.

10. Engine — Ratio (MQAE)

On screen: "Master Quantitative Action Engine — the 'Ratio' entry filter".

In one line: five independent models each score the PCR line and the ratio line. The scores are added up, and if the total clears a threshold, that is the vote.
This engine previews your edits live. Unlike the others, the scores and chart update as you type — but "Live trading still uses the saved config until you Save."

10.1 Execution Risk Profile

AGGRESSIVE MODEEarly entry. Velocity & Order Blocks weighted ×3. Sets the threshold to 3. (Default.)
CONSERVATIVE MODESafe entry. SMC & Crossover weighted ×3. Sets the threshold to 6.

They are mutually exclusive — you cannot switch both off; clicking the active one does nothing. Switching mode rewrites the threshold, but you can still edit it afterwards.

10.2 The five models

Signal Synthesis shows three pills — Green (PCR) Net, Yellow (Ratio) Net, Crossover — then the verdict box (CALL EXECUTION / PUT EXECUTION / NO TRADE) and "Total +7 vs ±3 threshold".

11. Engine — Ultra Master Pro

On screen: "NIFTY Ultra Master Pro — execution engine (entries + all exits)".

In one line: this engine never votes on direction. Once the filters say CALL or PUT, it maps important price levels, waits for a precise setup, enters — and from then on it alone decides when to exit.

11.1 Data-Ready Gate

Four dots — {N}m feed · Daily feed (2/3) · Weekly feed · 1H feed — green when satisfied. Then the verdict: GATE OPEN — entries permitted or GATE CLOSED — entries blocked until all feeds resolve.

A closed gate is the single most common reason a fresh contract takes no trades. The engine needs enough history to build its levels, and it refuses to guess.

11.2 Strike selector

HUNTED — in bandIts parallel multi-strike set, nearest band-middle first.
ALL STRIKESAny contract on the chain, for manual inspection.

A badge shows which you are on: BAND, MANUAL, or ATM fallback (no strike currently sits inside the band). View only — it never changes config.

11.3 Entry Model State Machine

Shows IN TRADE / WATCHING / idle, and when in a trade the sub-scenario, entry price and base level.

"Retest entries (R1/R2) fire only on Structural, Discovery & Bridge levels — Median is excluded (synthetic, lower confidence). The filter layer opens the door; only price action (or the day's End-Exit) closes it."

12. Paper Trading

Paper trading means practice trading. Every order goes to a simulator instead of the broker. Same engines, same rules, same fees — only the execution is pretend. It is how you test without risking money.

12.2 Simulated Order Execution

Entry priceThe close of the minute that triggered the entry, plus slippage. The engine's signal fires when a candle touches a level (say ₹195), but the system only knows about that candle once it closes — and the close (say ₹198) is the earliest price a real order can get. The chart marker still shows the ₹195 signal; the trade list shows the real ₹198 entry. Paper, backtest and live all use this rule.
Slippage (%)Default 0.5. Always applied against you, as in real life.
Simulated latency (ms)Default 250, capped at 2000. In live paper trading the simulator really waits this long before committing the fill.
Fill sourceRead-only: "Live LTP (OI Algo feed)". The feed stores last-traded-price only, so bid/ask mid cannot be simulated.
Backtests always use deterministic minute-close fills — latency is recorded but never simulated there. Fees are identical for paper and live, so both always speak the same costs.

"Breakeven: the premium must move ₹X in your favour just to cover charges."

Rates come live from the Integrations tab — edit them there and this calculator follows.

17. Decision Trace

One row per evaluated minute — the single best tool for answering "why did nothing happen?"

Filter pills: all · reject · accept · with candidates. Columns: Time · Zone · Stage · Dir · Readings · Cands · Age · Decision · Reason.

Click any row to expand it. You then see every engine's full working for that minute:

  • OI Structure — call/put points, confidence, the OI deltas, breakouts, and a chip per scoring event.
  • MTF Ratio — the full timeframe table, then each rule with ✔ / ✗ / ○ and the reason it passed or failed.
  • MQAE — the total, the green/yellow/crossover split, and one chip per model.
  • Candidates — every strike considered, whether it was in band, whether the engine was ready, and which one fired.
  • Footer — data age, whether the engines agreed, config version, sizing arithmetic, and the zone's exact rule settings.
If you only learn one diagnostic screen, learn this one. Every "why didn't it trade?" question is answered here, minute by minute, with the evidence attached.

Part Two — Backtesting

Backtesting means replaying history. The system takes your exact settings and runs the same engine code over stored past data, minute by minute, to show what would have happened. It is how you test an idea before risking anything.

19. The workspace

27. Glossary

PremiumThe price of one unit of the option contract.
StrikeThe price level an option is written at, e.g. 23600.
CE / PECall option / Put option.
ATM"At the money" — the strike nearest the current index level.
OI (Open Interest)How many contracts are currently held open. Rising OI means new money entering.
PCRPut-Call Ratio — put OI divided by call OI.
LotOptions trade in fixed bundles. One NIFTY lot is 65 units.
BandThe premium range a zone will consider. Contracts outside it are ignored.
HuntThe engine watching a chosen strike, waiting for its entry setup.
UnanimousEvery switched-on engine with a direction agrees.
SlippageThe gap between the price you expected and the price you got. Always modelled against you.
DrawdownThe fall from a peak to the following trough. The usual measure of "how bad did it get".
LedgerWhich book a trade belongs to — PAPER or LIVE. They are kept entirely separate.
Three habits worth forming.
1. Hover before you guess — almost every control explains itself.
2. Read the live sentence under a setting after you change it; several of them recalculate and will tell you if you have done something unintended.
3. When something does not trade, open the Decision Trace or the Entry Funnel before changing anything. The platform almost always already knows why.
